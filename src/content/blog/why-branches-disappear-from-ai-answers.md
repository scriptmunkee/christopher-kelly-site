Most multi-location brands assume their visibility in AI search looks like their visibility in Google: strong for the brand, decent for most branches. Run the questions customers actually ask, branch by branch, and the picture is usually very different. A handful of locations get named again and again. The rest barely appear.

## The Short Answer

AI assistants don't rank; they shortlist. Ask for "the best gym near me" or "a vet open on Saturdays" and the answer names two or three businesses, often with a reason for each. A branch that misses that shortlist isn't on page two. It's not there at all.

The map pack and the AI answer don't always agree, either. In a [Local SEO Data study](https://martechseries.com/predictive-ai/ai-platforms-machine-learning/local-seo-data-study-finds-28-5-of-local-pack-businesses-are-invisible-in-google-ai-mode/) of 1,120 US queries, 28.5% of businesses in Google's Local Pack didn't appear in AI Mode for the same question. Doing well in local SEO is no guarantee of being named.

## Five Reasons Branches Go Missing

### 1. Their details conflict across the web

AI pieces a branch together from your site, Google Business Profile, Apple Maps, Bing Places, directories and reviews. When those sources disagree, AI either guesses or plays safe and names someone else.

The error rates are high. Non-Google AI assistants were only about 68% accurate on business contact details in [SOCi's 2026 Local Visibility Index](https://golocal.soci.ai/rs/355-TUF-572/images/The_2026_Local_Visibility_Index_SOCi100.pdf). In a US study of 10,000 businesses by [Insites](https://insites.com/blog/does-chatgpt-hallucinate-local-businesses-the-data-on-ai-accuracy/), ChatGPT's details matched Google Business Profile for only 56%, and the phone number was wrong for 30%.

### 2. The location page is thin or duplicated

Many brands run location pages that are the same template with a different address. AI has nothing to say about a page like that. A strong location page states what that branch offers, who it serves, its opening hours, its team and the questions local customers ask.

This matters because AI leans on what brands publish. [Yext's analysis](https://yext.com/about/news-media/ai-citations-release) of 6.8 million AI citations found most come from sources brands control: their websites and their listings. A weak location page wastes the source AI trusts most.

### 3. Reviews are old, few or uneven

Recent reviews carry the most weight with customers, and AI summarises them. [BrightLocal found](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) 74% of consumers only look at reviews from the last three months, and 82% read AI-generated review summaries. A branch with a burst of reviews from two years ago looks less current than a competitor with a steady flow.

### 4. Nobody else mentions the branch

Local press, community sites, "best of" round-ups and partner pages give AI independent evidence that a branch exists and is worth recommending. Flagship locations often have plenty. Smaller branches often have none.

### 5. Reporting only looks at the brand

This is why the problem goes unnoticed. A dashboard that tracks "brand visibility in AI" can look healthy while most branches are absent. The flagship carries the average. Until you measure branch by branch, you can't see which locations are missing, or why.

## How to Fix It

### Step 1: Baseline every branch

Build a fixed set of customer questions for each location type and market, such as "near me", "best in [town]", service questions and availability. Run them through ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI Mode, and record whether each branch is mentioned, cited with a link, or missing, and who is named instead. That's what an [SEO and AI visibility audit](https://christopher-kelly.co.uk/services/seo-ai-visibility-audit/) covers.

### Step 2: Create one source of truth

Choose one system as the master record for every branch's name, address, phone, hours and services. Push it to every profile and directory, and audit for drift regularly. At portfolio scale this is worth automating.

### Step 3: Give every branch a real page

Unique content per location: services, team, opening hours, parking and access, prices or "from" prices, and answers to local questions. Add LocalBusiness structured data for each branch, linked to your organisation.

### Step 4: Run a steady, compliant review programme

Ask every customer, not just happy ones, and respond consistently. Fake, incentivised or selectively gathered reviews are banned under the DMCC Act, with [CMA guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fake-reviews-cma208) on what that means in practice, and Google's own policies prohibit them too.

### Step 5: Keep profile names clean

Use each branch's real-world name on Google Business Profile. Adding keywords or locations to the name breaks [Google's guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177?hl=en) and risks suspension, which removes the branch from the map and from AI answers built on it.

### Step 6: Measure monthly, per branch

Track the same questions every month and report by branch, region and brand.

| Measure | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| Mentioned | Whether AI names the branch at all |
| Cited with a link | Whether AI uses your own page as its source |
| Missing | Which branches and questions to fix first |
| Accuracy | Whether hours, phone and address in AI answers are correct |
| Competitors named | Who wins the shortlist when you don't |

## Where to Start

Pick ten branches: your best performers and your weakest. Run twenty real customer questions for each. The gap between them usually points straight at the cause, whether that's conflicting data, a thin page or a review drought.
