This case comes from my multi-year work with a global luxury hotel group, which is named on request. It's a small project with an outsized lesson, because the same problem affects any brand whose name, location or product overlaps with something more famous.

## The Problem: A Name Shared With Somewhere Else

The resort sits in a beautiful, relatively little-known region. Its location terms, though, are shared with a much better-known town in another country.

For search engines and AI answers, that overlap was a constant source of confusion. When travellers asked about the destination, answers blended the two places. They got the wrong context, the wrong directions and, at times, facts that belonged to a different country entirely.

This is an entity problem, not a ranking problem. AI engines don't just match words; they try to work out which real-world thing a question is about. When two things share a name and one has far more written about it, the better-known one wins by default, and its facts leak into answers about the other.

## Why It Matters More in AI Search

In traditional search, a confused result is one blue link among ten. A traveller can scroll past it.

In an AI answer there is often one response. If that response mixes up two places, the traveller reads the mistake as fact. For a luxury resort, where the destination is a large part of what guests are buying, being described as somewhere else is a direct commercial risk.

## What We Changed

We re-anchored the entity to its true region. In practice that meant three things.

### 1. Clearer location facts on the page

We built a dedicated destination page for the resort. It states plainly where the destination is: the region, the country and the nearby places that only exist there. Those details belong only to the real destination, so they separate it from its namesake.

### 2. Consistent regional details everywhere AI looks

A page alone isn't enough if the rest of the web tells a different story. We aligned the regional details across the resort's profiles and its structured data, so every source AI might read describes the same place in the same way.

### 3. Answer-first getting-there content

Travellers' most common questions about a remote destination are practical: where is it, and how do I get there? We answered those directly, at the top of the page, in short factual sentences. That is exactly the format AI engines lift into an answer.

## The Result

Google's AI Overview now cites the resort's destination page as both **the definition of the destination** and **the getting-there source**. Instead of borrowing facts from the more famous town, the answer is built from the resort's own official page.

## How to Tell if Your Brand Has the Same Problem

Name collisions are more common than most brands realise. Watch for these signs:

- **AI answers mix in facts that aren't yours.** Opening hours, locations, founders or products that belong to a different organisation.
- **Your brand name is also a place, a common word or another company's name.** The more famous meaning wins unless you give AI clear evidence.
- **Questions about you return answers about the wrong country or region.** Particularly common for hotels, resorts and multi-location brands in places with shared names.
- **Your own site is missing from the answer, even though it ranks.** AI may have decided your page is about something else.

The same approach works beyond hospitality. It's the core of [entity and knowledge graph optimisation](https://christopher-kelly.co.uk/services/#entity-optimisation).

1. **Run the questions.** Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity and Google AI the questions your customers ask, and note every fact that belongs to someone else.
2. **Define the entity on your own site.** One page that states plainly what you are, where you are and what makes you distinct, using details only you have.
3. **Make every source agree.** Business profiles, directories and structured data should describe the same entity with the same facts.
4. **Answer the practical questions first.** Short, factual answers at the top of the page are what AI quotes.
5. **Track it over time.** Re-run the same questions monthly, and record whether you're mentioned, cited or missing.
