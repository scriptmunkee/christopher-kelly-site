## Wednesday Evening, After the Bell

On Wednesday 28 October, after the US market closed, Alphabet published its third-quarter results.

On paper, it was a record. [Revenue of $127.6 billion](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/2026q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf), up 25%. Google Cloud more than doubled. Sundar Pichai told analysts that Search queries were growing at [their fastest rate in five years](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-alphabet-q3-2026-profit-miss-sparks-10-afterhours-drop-93CH-4981037).

Within minutes, in after-hours trading, the shares were down 10%.

By Thursday's close, Class A shares were down 12.6% at $283.82. About [$500 billion of value had gone](https://www.foxbusiness.com/markets/google-parent-alphabet-suffers-nearly-500b-wall-street-wipeout-amid-ai-spending-fears) in a single session. It was Alphabet's worst day ever, and the second-largest one-day loss in stock market history, behind only Nvidia's $589 billion in January 2025.

So what did investors see that the headline missed?

## The Line Everyone Was Reading

They were reading one line: Google Search.

Search and other revenue grew 12% to $63.35 billion. That sounds healthy until you see the trend. Growth was [19% in Q1 and about 17% in Q2](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/alphabet-just-lost-500-billion-203300362.html). From Q2 to Q3 it barely moved: [up about 0.1%](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/10/alphabet-q3-2026-search-ad-growth-slowed-queries-did-not/), against 4.4% for the same step a year earlier.

More people were searching than ever. Google was earning less growth from each search.

Philipp Schindler, Google's chief business officer, gave several reasons. Currency was the biggest. A strong World Cup quarter was hard to repeat. And then this, in his words: "the strong growth in AI Mode queries had an impact because of the lower monetization we were seeing in AI Mode."

That's the sentence that matters if you run a website.

## Now Look at the Same Quarter From Your Side

Google earns money when someone clicks an ad. You earn money when someone clicks through to you. Both depend on one thing: a person leaving the results page.

Here is what the quarter says about that.

**People are asking longer questions.** Pichai said AI Mode queries are, on average, three times longer than traditional searches. One in five uses voice, images or documents. These aren't "running shoes". They're "which running shoes for a heavy runner with flat feet who trains on roads".

**The answer comes back on Google's page.** AI Mode and AI Overviews write the answer. They pick a few sources and name a few brands. For most questions, that's where the journey ends.

**Clicks are not keeping up with questions.** Paid clicks [rose 8%](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/10/alphabet-q3-2026-search-ad-growth-slowed-queries-did-not/) while queries grew at a five-year high. Google doesn't report organic clicks. But if Google itself is getting fewer paid clicks per search, it would be optimistic to assume your organic clicks per search are holding up better.

This isn't new. In the US, [68% of Google searches already ended without a click](https://searchengineland.com/google-zero-click-searches-2026-study-479717) earlier this year (SparkToro data, US only). What's new is that it now shows up in Alphabet's own revenue line.

| What Google reported | What it means for your traffic |
|---|---|
| Fastest query growth in five years | Demand for answers isn't falling. It's moving |
| AI Mode queries three times longer | Buyers ask detailed, specific questions, and AI answers them in full |
| AI Mode earns less per query | More of the journey ends on Google's page, without a click |
| Paid clicks up 8%, slower than queries | Expect fewer clicks per search, organic as well as paid |
| Ads now scaling into AI Overviews and AI Mode | Paid placements will compete inside the answer, not just above it |

## Google's Side of the Story

To be fair to Google, this is one quarter, and a messy one.

Most of the fall in profit came from an [$8.2 billion paper loss](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-alphabet-q3-2026-profit-miss-sparks-10-afterhours-drop-93CH-4981037) on Alphabet's SpaceX stake, not from Search. Currency and the World Cup comparison are temporary. Investors were also nervous about cost: [capital spending hit $51.4 billion](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q3/2026q3-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) in the quarter, more than double a year ago, and free cash flow was negative $9.1 billion.

Google's leadership calls AI an expansion, not a threat. Pichai described agentic search as "an expansion opportunity", and ads are now rolling out inside AI Overviews and AI Mode in more countries. If those ads earn like classic search ads, the gap could close.

Not everyone is convinced. Mike O'Rourke of JonesTrading told the Wall Street Journal that Google [showed vulnerability to AI](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/alphabet-just-lost-500-billion-203300362.html). Another analyst said the miss raises fears about AI and agentic tools [taking share from Search and YouTube](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/alphabet-loses-500-billion-market-224227341.html).

Either way, the direction for brands is the same. Google is redesigning search around answers. The question is whether those answers include you.

## What I'd Do About It This Quarter

### 1. Find out where you stand in the answers

Rankings and clicks won't tell you this on their own. Take the 30 to 50 questions your buyers really ask and run them through Google AI Mode, AI Overviews, ChatGPT and Gemini. Record whether you're named, whether your page is cited with a link, and who appears instead. That baseline is the first step of an [SEO and AI visibility audit](https://christopher-kelly.co.uk/services/seo-ai-visibility-audit/).

### 2. Stop writing what everyone else writes

"What is X?" and "How much does X cost?" are the questions AI now answers itself, from a dozen near-identical articles. That content isn't worthless. It still captures demand today. But it's the first to lose clicks.

The content AI can't write for itself is the content only you can write. What you did, for whom, and what changed. Why your product or service is different. The decisions you made and the evidence behind them. Write it the way you'd brief a sharp new customer, because that's effectively who's reading: a system deciding whether to recommend you.

### 3. Make your brand impossible to misread

AI recommends what it understands. Your name, locations, services and prices should say the same thing on your site, your Google Business Profile, your listings and your structured data. When sources disagree, AI hedges, or names someone else. I've seen a [resort become Google's AI Overview source for its own destination](https://christopher-kelly.co.uk/blog/resort-ai-overview-case-study/) once its location facts stopped conflicting.

### 4. Measure mentions and citations, not just clicks

If more journeys end inside the answer, clicks will undercount your visibility. Track, every month, whether you're mentioned, whether you're cited, and whether the answer about you is accurate. The [AI search statistics for 2026](https://christopher-kelly.co.uk/blog/ai-search-statistics-2026/) show how fast buyers are making decisions that way.

## The Takeaway

Google's search engine has never been busier. It is just keeping more of the journey for itself.

That cost Alphabet $500 billion in a day. For most brands, the cost will be slower and quieter: a few fewer clicks every month, while the questions keep growing.

The brands that come through it won't be the ones with the most pages. They'll be the ones AI understands, quotes and names.
